# Content Strategy — Meditation VR Web App

## Project
Meditation VR Web App — waiting-room browser VR prototype

## Content Lead
Content Lead

## Emotional Target
The first demo should create a feeling of:

- Creative clarity
- Calm
- Safety
- Gentle hope
- Spaciousness without pressure

The experience should feel supportive, non-clinical, and easy to enter. Users may be in a waiting room, preparing for care, reflecting on goals, or simply needing a calm reset.

## Content Goals

### Primary Goal
Guide the user through a short, soothing meditation-style experience that helps them feel safe, grounded, and creatively clear.

### Secondary Goals
- Offer affirmations that feel warm and believable.
- Encourage visualization without forcing emotional vulnerability.
- Support a vision-board style reflection using gentle prompts.
- Keep the language accessible for users who may be stressed, distracted, or new to meditation.
- Avoid therapeutic claims, diagnosis language, or clinical promises.

## First-Session Narrative Arc

The first demo should follow this flow:

1. **Safe Arrival**
   - Welcome the user.
   - Reassure them that there is no right way to participate.
   - Invite them to settle in and notice the environment.

2. **Calming Reset**
   - Offer short grounding cues.
   - Encourage slow breathing and physical ease.
   - Use language that supports emotional safety.

3. **Creative Clarity Visualization**
   - Invite the user to imagine a peaceful future moment.
   - Prompt them to notice colors, textures, symbols, and feelings.
   - Encourage curiosity instead of performance.

4. **Vision-Board Reflection**
   - Provide image-generation style prompts or visual inspiration cards.
   - Allow the user to select or preview calming themes.
   - Keep controls simple and non-disruptive.

5. **Affirmation Close**
   - End with short affirmations focused on choice, inner steadiness, and clarity.
   - Invite the user to carry one image, word, or feeling forward.

## Tone of Voice

### Voice Attributes
- Calm
- Spacious
- Reassuring
- Respectful
- Gentle
- Imaginative
- Non-clinical
- Plainspoken

### Avoid
- Urgency
- Overly spiritual language
- Medical or therapeutic guarantees
- Diagnostic language
- Pressure to feel better
- Shame-based motivation
- Complex instructions
- Placeholder or filler copy

### Preferred Language
Use:
- “You may…”
- “If it feels comfortable…”
- “Let your attention rest…”
- “There is no need to force anything…”
- “Notice what feels steady…”
- “Choose the image that feels supportive…”

Avoid:
- “You must…”
- “Fix your anxiety…”
- “Heal your trauma…”
- “Unlock your full potential instantly…”
- “This treatment will…”

## Audience Considerations

The user may be:
- Waiting for an appointment
- Feeling mildly anxious or uncertain
- Seeking calm before a conversation
- Interested in personal growth or creativity
- Using a phone, tablet, desktop, or headset-enabled browser
- New to VR-like web experiences

Content should not assume:
- Prior meditation experience
- Comfort with VR
- A specific spiritual belief system
- A diagnosis
- A specific reason for being in the waiting room

## Content Principles

### 1. Safety Before Depth
The demo should not push users toward intense memories or emotional processing. Keep prompts present-focused, choice-based, and gentle.

### 2. Clarity Over Poetry
Use beautiful language, but prioritize comprehension. Short, simple sentences are best.

### 3. Choice-Based Guidance
Give users permission to participate in their own way.

### 4. Non-Clinical Support
The app can feel therapeutic without claiming to provide therapy.

### 5. Low Cognitive Load
Avoid long paragraphs during the experience. Use short lines, calm pacing, and predictable controls.

## Recommended Content Surfaces

### Home / Arrival Screen
Purpose:
- Introduce the experience.
- Set expectations.
- Offer a clear start button.

Recommended copy:
> A quiet space for calm, safety, and creative clarity.  
> Settle in, breathe gently, and let supportive images come into focus.

Primary CTA:
> Begin

Secondary CTA:
> Preview the Journey

### Guided Experience Panel
Purpose:
- Show one short prompt or affirmation at a time.
- Keep users oriented in the session arc.

Prompt format:
- Title: 2–5 words
- Body: 1–2 short sentences
- Optional affirmation: 1 sentence

### Vision Board Area
Purpose:
- Let users choose calming visualization themes.
- Provide prompt cards that can inspire generated or simulated imagery.

Theme examples:
- Soft Morning Light
- Safe Garden Path
- Clear Blue Horizon
- Creative Studio
- Floating Lanterns
- Quiet Forest Room

### Closing Screen
Purpose:
- End gently.
- Encourage carry-forward reflection.

Recommended copy:
> Take one word, image, or feeling with you.  
> You can return to this quiet space whenever you need a moment of clarity.

CTA:
> Begin Again

## Accessibility Content Requirements

- Buttons must have clear labels.
- Avoid relying on color alone to communicate state.
- Provide text alternatives for visual cards where possible.
- Keep motion language optional and gentle.
- Avoid flashing, startling, or high-intensity visual descriptions.
- Use plain language for controls.

## Required Implementation Copy Rules

The implementation must not include:
- Lorem ipsum
- TODO copy
- “Coming soon”
- “Placeholder”
- “Here could be”
- Empty cards
- Dead buttons
- Links that navigate outside the preview frame unless explicitly required

All visible copy should be final-quality demo copy.
